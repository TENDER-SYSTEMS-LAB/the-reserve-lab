# Institutional Directions

All five directions below were proposed by ChatGPT. No selection by the user appears in this source. They are alternatives for one bank artwork, not five approved projects or a roadmap.

| Direction | Institutional action and central tension | Proposed mechanisms | Main risk identified in the source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Future Claims Bank | Assess which promises can support present credit; bind the future self through a present contract | Underwriting, discounting, collateral, maturity, default, refinancing | Turning time or the future into a literal commodity or science-fiction device |
| Credibility Institute | Evaluate a submitted claim; ask how much evidence trust can demand before it ceases to be trust | Verification, claim ratings, guarantees, performance history | Scoring whole people or reproducing a social-credit dystopia |
| Custody Institution | Protect something by taking control of access; ask what preservation requires surrendering | Lock-up, withdrawal limits, redemption, pooling, liquidity and run risk | A generic memory bank, or duplicating an ownership-centered artwork |
| Clearing House of Obligations | Record and net obligations; ask which debts can truly be settled | Bilateral/multilateral netting, reconciliation, settlement finality, counterparty risk | Abstraction that makes the interaction difficult to grasp |
| Guarantee / Last Resort Institution | Stand behind promises and contain their failure; ask who guarantees the guarantor | Coverage, reserves, collateral, default funds, contagion and stress testing | Starting at an infrastructure scale too remote from the visitor |

## Recommendation in the source

ChatGPT ranks Future Claims Bank strongest, linking a promise, eligibility assessment, present credit, time, and contractual consequences. [[future-claims]] preserves that proposal in more detail. The source also describes clearing as potentially the most distinctive independent artwork; this does not override its explicit overall recommendation.

## Evolution — audit comparison without selecting a type

The 2026-09-06 design audit develops a different test of institutional necessity: identify the visitor's present benefit or delegated control, the rights and obligations created, the institution's own responsibility, and the conditions for ending that relationship. A balance, rejection screen, elapsed time, or account history alone does not distinguish the work.

The audit proposes future claims as the first test candidate, with custody as the nearest alternative if control and return produce the stronger experience. This comparison is not a user adoption or rejection of any type. The approved bio does not rank the candidates.

| Candidate | Proposed test before choosing it | Boundary that changes if chosen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Future claims | A specific future performance commitment enables a meaningful present action; identify the beneficiary, accepting party, and both sides' obligations | Define the present right, eligibility, performance, maturity, cancellation, and closure before quantities or credit calculations |
| Custody | Delegating access or control produces a consequential return obligation, beyond storage and retrieval | Separate the object, access right, custody record, delegated powers, and return conditions |
| Credibility | A judgment changes an actual permission, guarantee, or responsibility | A score or research output alone does not establish the need for a bank form |
| Clearing | Multiple obligations can or cannot be extinguished under consistent rules that the visitor can understand | Define parties, comparability, consent to closure, and unresolved differences; a shared currency is not automatically required |
| Guarantee / last resort | The institution actually assumes a consequence of another party's failure | Define the guarantee, resources, limits, and guarantor failure; expand the scope if this is the core question |

These are artistic selection tests, not legal classifications of real financial institutions. [[future-claims]] holds the provisional interaction test; [[worldbuilding-roadmap]] compares worldbuilding scopes and their conditional costs.
